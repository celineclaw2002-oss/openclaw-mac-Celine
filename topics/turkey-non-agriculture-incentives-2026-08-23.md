# Turkey Non-Agriculture Incentives Research - 2026-08-23

## What we decided

- Screen Turkish non-agriculture subsidy and incentive frameworks for a first-time investor with around `EUR 500k` equivalent.
- Prioritize official Turkish sources and focus on sectors where incentives can matter at this size:
  - light manufacturing
  - tourism
  - renewable or energy-adjacent uses
  - logistics
  - tech and service exports
- Exclude agriculture-specific programs.

## What we found

- The main anchor is the national investment incentive framework:
  - `Yatırımlarda Devlet Yardımları Hakkında Karar` (`2025/9903`)
  - operationally accessed through `Yatırım Teşvik Belgesi`
  - administered via `E-TUYS`
- For this budget, the realistic sweet spot is not mega-project incentives. It is:
  - `Target Incentives` / regional-sectoral investment certificate use
  - possibly `Priority` treatment only in selected cases
  - plus sector overlays such as KOSGEB, free zones, TDZ/technopark rules, and Ministry of Trade service-export supports
- Official threshold examples gathered from `yatirimadestek.gov.tr` Q&A:
  - example priority investment in a 4th-region case: minimum `7.5 million TL`
  - example target investment in Izmir (1st region) manufacturing-style case: minimum `15.1 million TL`
- Core investment-certificate support instruments confirmed from official sources:
  - VAT exemption on machinery/equipment
  - customs duty exemption
  - corporate tax reduction / tax discount
  - employer social security premium support
  - employee social security premium support in stronger cases
  - income tax withholding support in stronger cases
  - interest/profit-share support
  - land allocation
  - VAT exemption on construction in some frameworks
- `E-TUYS` application friction is real:
  - qualified e-signature required
  - ministry authorization required
  - authorization requests handled via `KEP`
  - practical newcomer barrier is administrative setup plus NACE/eligibility classification

## Program-level takeaways

- `Yatırım Teşvik Belgesi` under the investment incentive system:
  - best fit for capex-heavy light manufacturing
  - can also matter for formal tourism projects
  - may help some energy-adjacent capex tied to a main operating business
- `KOSGEB Girişimci Destek Programı`:
  - useful for small startup build-out
  - official page showed `20,000 TL` company establishment support, staff support, and up to `1.5 million TL` repayable project support
  - meaningful as a supplement, not as the main reason to invest `EUR 500k`
- `KOSGEB Kapasite Geliştirme Destek Programı`:
  - stronger for an already-operating SME in manufacturing, telecom, software, data, or R&D-related NACE classes
  - official page showed `1 million TL` minimum and generally `20 million TL` upper credit limit, with higher ceilings in defense-linked cases
  - less realistic for a true newcomer without an operating track record
- `KOSGEB Küresel Rekabetçilik Destek Programı`:
  - clearly aimed at fast-growing / export-rising / tech firms
  - likely poor fit for a first-time investor starting from zero
- Ministry of Trade service-export framework:
  - official `Hizmet Sektörü Destekleri` page links the current framework decision and implementation generalge
  - attractive for software, digital services, business services, education, health tourism, and similar exporters
  - best used when the business model earns export revenue from services rather than local demand
- `Hizmet Sektörleri Atılım Programı`:
  - official sector pages show support heads such as platform commission, software license, digital product promotion, report/database membership, workforce development, and various shared promotion/commercialization items
  - promising for SaaS, gaming, digital intermediation, consulting, and exportable service models
- `Yurt Dışı Lojistik Dağıtım Ağları Programı`:
  - official pages show support buckets for employment, promotion/site visits, satellite warehouse rent, and setup/operating expenses
  - strong only if the logistics plan is already cross-border and structured enough to justify overseas network build-out
- `Technology Development Zones` / technoparks:
  - official Investment Office page confirms tax and VAT advantages through `2028` for software/R&D/design activity plus payroll tax relief and partial employer social-security support
  - good fit only for genuine R&D/software work, not plain commercial operations
- `Free Zones`:
  - official Investment Office page confirms very strong export-oriented exemptions, including customs, VAT, and manufacturing corporate tax relief, plus some logistics-service tax relief
  - potentially very attractive for export manufacturing or export logistics, but operational setup is more complex than a standard domestic SME launch

## Practical verdicts

- Best fit:
  - light manufacturing using a `Yatırım Teşvik Belgesi`, ideally in a location and NACE category where target/regional supports apply
  - export-oriented software or digital services using Ministry of Trade service-export supports, optionally with TDZ structure if the work is truly R&D/software-led
  - niche export manufacturing or logistics in a free zone, but only if export intensity is genuinely high from day one
- Conditional fit:
  - formal hotel/tourism project if the budget is enough to reach the regional threshold after land/building/refit costs
  - energy-adjacent capex as part of a broader industrial or hospitality project, not as a clean standalone small-investor play from the sources reviewed
- Weak fit:
  - KOSGEB global-competitiveness style programs for a brand-new entrant
  - overseas logistics-network programs unless the investor already has export flow and operating partners
  - large strategic/priority mega-investment frameworks

## Open questions / risks

- Exact NACE matching and threshold treatment can change by investment subject and region.
- Several Ministry of Trade pages expose support categories cleanly, but exact rates and caps may require reading linked PDF generalges or current application guides case by case.
- Renewable standalone economics were not confirmed as a clean small-investor incentive play from the reviewed sources; the safer conclusion is that energy-related support is more compelling when embedded inside a larger operating business.
