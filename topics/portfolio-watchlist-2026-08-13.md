## Healthcare diversification follow-up

- Can asked whether healthcare and tech/semis are negatively correlated such that healthcare tends to rise when tech falls.
- Conclusion: the clean version of that claim is too strong.
- Better framing: healthcare is typically a defensive sector with low-to-moderate correlation to tech, not a consistently inverse trade.
- Recent supporting references found:
  - BlackRock framed healthcare as having relatively low correlation with US tech and called it a portfolio complement rather than an outright hedge.
  - Investopedia, citing UBS/Bespoke in June 2026, noted healthcare outperformed the S&P 500 on 85% of days when the index was down 1% or more, which supports defensive behavior during market stress.
  - Several 2026 rotation pieces described money moving from semis/tech into healthcare during risk-off periods, but also showed the relationship can reverse when growth leadership returns.
- Portfolio implication for this book: healthcare can make sense as ballast against a very AI/semis-heavy setup, but it should be treated as diversification / lower-beta growth or defensiveness, not as a guaranteed short-term inverse pair trade.
- If adding healthcare for this purpose, higher-quality large-cap pharma, managed care, or broad healthcare ETFs are likely better fits than high-beta biotech.
