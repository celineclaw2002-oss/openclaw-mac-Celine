# Italy Agriculture-First Property Screen

## Brief

Search for Italian rural properties with:

- maximum purchase price `EUR 500,000`
- a real house or habitable building already present
- agricultural revenue as the primary investment thesis
- limited additional capex required
- plausibly manageable by hired local labor / farm contractors
- target breakeven window of roughly `5-10 years`

## Core conclusion

After screening public listings, my honest conclusion is:

- I did **not** find a large set of clean, obvious agriculture-only winners under `EUR 500k`
- most listings fail because they are too lifestyle-heavy, too restoration-heavy, or too thin on productive acreage
- the **closest fits** are concentrated in:
  - Marche / Umbria for turnkey small vineyards
  - south-eastern Sicily for low entry price irrigated citrus/olive combinations

The public market appears to have a structural problem:

- habitable house + meaningful productive land + low capex + sub-`EUR 500k` rarely all show up together

## How I filtered

I rejected listings that had one or more of these features:

1. house value dominates the economics
2. land looks pretty but has weak income density
3. property requires a major rebuild or large recovery spend
4. crop mix is too small to support hired maintenance
5. no evidence of current production or irrigation
6. price is only justifiable with hospitality, not farming

## Operating benchmarks used

These are rough underwriting anchors, not certainties:

- grape-growing net contribution:
  - basic bulk-grape case: `EUR 3.5k/ha`
  - stronger turnkey / DOC / organic case: up to `EUR 5k/ha`
- olive net contribution:
  - around `EUR 2.0k-2.5k/ha` for reasonable productive groves
- citrus net contribution:
  - around `EUR 2.5k-2.8k/ha` in mature irrigated orchards based on Italian citrus cost / yield references
- arable net contribution:
  - around `EUR 0.45k-0.5k/ha`
- direct support:
  - roughly `EUR 250/ha`

These benchmarks already assume outsourced farm work is part of the cultivation cost base.

## 1. Chiaramonte Gulfi, Sicily

### Listing

- URL: `https://www.immobiliare.it/annunci/127807360/`
- price: `EUR 205,000`
- updated: `2026-05-05`

### What the listing says

- detached house of about `110 sqm`
- about `6 ha` of land
- orange grove in full production
- olive grove in full production
- drilled wells and artificial pond / water reserve
- no obvious immediate structural disaster in the house

### Why it stands out

This is the strongest price-to-productive-land ratio I found **with a house already present**.

Why it screens well:

- low entry price
- productive irrigated southern crop mix
- citrus is materially more attractive than weak traditional olive-only land
- water infrastructure is specifically highlighted

### Main risk

- exact split between citrus and olives is not specified
- citrus is more operationally sensitive than casual olive ownership
- remote owner must rely on a trustworthy local operator

### Investment view

This is the **best pure screening candidate** from the search.

It is not guaranteed to hit a true 5-year discounted breakeven, but relative to the market it is the closest thing I found to a property where agriculture, not the house, drives the value.

### Verdict

- `best fit`
- merits direct follow-up with seller / agent

## 2. Acquaviva Picena, Marche, Contrada Forola

### Listing

- URL: `https://www.immobiliare.it/annunci/123347679/`
- price: `EUR 420,000`
- updated: `2025-09-23`

### What the listing says

- farmhouse in good maintenance condition
- about `184 sqm`
- about `55,000 sqm` total land
- roughly:
  - `21,000 sqm` passerina bio vines
  - `21,000 sqm` montepulciano bio vines
  - `7,000 sqm` sangiovese bio vines
  - `6,000 sqm` olive grove with `300+` trees
- young plantings, described as full production
- tractor, atomizer, and agricultural equipment included

### Why it stands out

This is one of the very few listings that looks like an actual working farm rather than a romantic rural house.

Good points:

- productive vines already planted
- biological positioning
- equipment included
- house already usable
- strong exposure and established agricultural zone

### Main risk

- purchase price is still fairly high versus agricultural-only earnings
- a lot depends on the actual economics of those vines:
  - grape sale contracts
  - yields
  - whether the buyer is acquiring a functioning operating setup or just land plus tools

### Investment view

This can work **better than Capannori**, but it is still not a slam dunk if the plan is passive ownership with someone else just “maintaining the land.”

It works best if:

- local management is tight
- the vines really are in full productive condition
- there is a stable route to selling grapes or wine

### Verdict

- `strong shortlist`
- probably closer to an `8-10 year` operational payback than a fast breakeven

## 3. Spello, Umbria, productive farm package

### Listing sources

- `https://www.casa.it/vendita/rustici-casali/spello/`
- corroborating search snippets from Idealista / Trovacasa
- price visible in snippet: `EUR 490,000`

### What the listing says

- productive farm transfer
- about `670 sqm`
- cellar
- warehouse
- agricultural equipment
- about `7.3 ha` of seminative land
- about `3 ha` vineyard
- about `2 ha` olive grove
- 2 artesian wells
- 1 additional well with strong flow
- `6 kW` photovoltaic system

### Why it stands out

This is arguably the most operationally complete sub-`EUR 500k` listing I found.

Why it matters:

- larger productive footprint than the Marches vineyard option
- mixed crops rather than one single crop bet
- existing farm infrastructure
- water availability appears unusually strong

### Main risk

- the price is near your ceiling
- this is more of a **business acquisition** than a simple land purchase
- if the economic value is mostly in an ongoing operation, diligence has to include:
  - production history
  - sales channels
  - equipment condition
  - legal structure of the transfer

### Investment view

Among the central-Italy options, this is one of the few that could justify itself on agricultural logic rather than hospitality optionality.

Still, it is not an effortless passive asset. It needs proper operator-level diligence.

### Verdict

- `strong shortlist`
- likely viable only if the farm business is genuinely turnkey

## Listings I screened out

I screened out many listings in Tuscany, Puglia and Lazio because they failed one of the following:

- house needed too much work
- acreage was too small for hired-management economics
- price depended on scenic or hospitality value
- crop mix was too thin
- no credible evidence of being operational already

That was especially common in:

- Tuscany under `EUR 500k`
- Puglia olive-house listings under `EUR 500k`

The recurring problem in Puglia was:

- nice habitable trullo or lamia
- small olive grove
- but not enough productive scale for attractive farm-only returns with hired maintenance

## Overall ranking

1. `Chiaramonte Gulfi, Sicily`
2. `Acquaviva Picena, Contrada Forola`
3. `Spello productive farm`
4. `Patti mixed farm`
5. `Monte San Giusto` as a borderline lifestyle-heavy option

## Most important takeaway

If you keep the full constraint set:

- Italy
- max `EUR 500k`
- existing house
- no large extra capex
- agriculture-first
- hired maintenance
- target `5-10 year` breakeven

then the market is **very tight**.

The best probabilities seem to come from:

1. lower-entry-price southern orchards with irrigation
2. small turnkey working vineyards in Marche / Umbria

not from classic Tuscan romantic farmhouses.

## Best next step

If continuing this search, I would now do one of these two:

1. go deep on the top `3-4` live candidates and contact agents for:
   - production history
   - exact yields
   - water data
   - labor setup
   - cadastral maps
   - tax / transfer structure

2. widen the net slightly by relaxing one constraint:
   - allow `no house but buildable`
   - or allow light but not major renovation

That would materially improve the opportunity set.
