# Capannori Agriculture-Only DCF

## Question

Could the property at Via Di Tofori 97 work as a productive investment at an entry price of `EUR 350,000` if the thesis is **agriculture only**, with no hospitality, no event business, and no heavy redevelopment thesis?

This note answers that with a 5-year and 10-year DCF built around:

- vineyard operations
- olive grove operations
- arable land use
- direct CAP-style support
- recurring holding and farm overhead
- recovery capex needed to restart neglected production
- exit value based on agricultural land value, not lifestyle or hospitality upside

## Short answer

My conclusion is:

- `EUR 350,000` is **too high for a base-case agriculture-only investment**
- it is **only defensible in a fairly optimistic operating case**
- if the goal is a solid risk-adjusted agricultural investment, the rough break-even entry price looks materially lower

At a `10%` discount rate:

- 5-year maximum purchase price
  - downside case: about `EUR 72k`
  - base case: about `EUR 189k`
  - high case: about `EUR 332k`
- 10-year maximum purchase price
  - downside case: effectively `not investable`
  - base case: about `EUR 129k`
  - high case: about `EUR 291k`

So under a serious agriculture-only underwriting, `EUR 350k` does **not** clear the bar unless operations materially outperform the base assumptions.

## Scope and framing

This model intentionally excludes:

- agriturismo
- weddings or events
- short-term rentals
- premium lifestyle resale value
- heavy restoration of the residential buildings

It also does not assume a branded winery or full hospitality-style retail platform.

I do allow for normal agricultural monetization:

- selling wine grapes
- producing and selling olive oil
- basic use of arable land
- CAP-style support payments if eligibility is achieved

## Property production base

Per the appraisal:

- vineyard: `3.055 ha`
- olive grove: `2.958 ha`
- woodland: `2.549 ha`
- arable land: `1.275 ha`
- total cadastral area: about `9.95 ha`

Relevant external operating anchors used in the model:

- Montecarlo DOC maximum yield cited at `100 q/ha` for white and `90 q/ha` for red
- Lucca-area land value references point roughly to:
  - vineyard: about `EUR 49k-75k/ha`
  - olive grove: about `EUR 18k-42k/ha`
- Italian CAP references suggest:
  - BISS average support around `EUR 168/ha`
  - redistributive support around `EUR 82/ha` for smaller hectare bands
- Tuscan / Italian olive support can be higher in qualifying olive-preservation programs, but that should be treated as upside rather than assumed base

These are anchors, not proof that this exact farm can achieve those numbers.

## Critical underwriting reality

The appraisal says the land is currently:

- not cultivated
- in poor maintenance / conservation condition
- tied to an abandoned/disused agricultural operation

That means a serious agriculture-only buyer should assume:

- recovery capex before normal production
- a ramp-up period before stable yields
- meaningful execution risk

This matters more than the headline hectare count.

## What drives value in an agriculture-only case

### Revenue sources included

1. vineyard revenue from grape production
2. olive revenue from oil production
3. arable land contribution
4. direct support payments
5. terminal resale value as agricultural land

### Costs included

1. purchase transaction costs
2. legal / technical diligence
3. vineyard recovery capex
4. olive grove recovery capex
5. irrigation / access / farm setup capex
6. annual cultivation costs
7. harvesting and outsourced mechanical work

## Main modeling assumptions

## 1. Recovery capex at acquisition

This is the biggest reason the agriculture-only case weakens.

I modeled one-time restart capex before the farm reaches normal output:

### Downside case

- vineyard recovery: `EUR 14k/ha`
- olive recovery: `EUR 7k/ha`
- site / irrigation / access / working setup: `EUR 32k`
- total initial agricultural capex: about `EUR 95k`

### Base case

- vineyard recovery: `EUR 11k/ha`
- olive recovery: `EUR 6k/ha`
- site / irrigation / access / working setup: `EUR 24k`
- total initial agricultural capex: about `EUR 75k`

### High case

- vineyard recovery: `EUR 9k/ha`
- olive recovery: `EUR 5k/ha`
- site / irrigation / access / working setup: `EUR 18k`
- total initial agricultural capex: about `EUR 60k`

These are meant to capture:

- pruning reset
- dead plant replacement
- trellis or row repair
- soil and fertility reset
- access clearing
- irrigation and drainage fixes
- startup agronomic work

## 2. Vineyard operating assumptions

I underwrote the vineyard as grape production only, not a fully integrated wine brand.

### Downside

- yield: `70 q/ha`
- realized grape price: `EUR 90/q`
- annual opex: `EUR 6,000/ha`
- annual vineyard contribution: about `EUR 0.9k`

### Base

- yield: `85 q/ha`
- realized grape price: `EUR 105/q`
- annual opex: `EUR 5,400/ha`
- annual vineyard contribution: about `EUR 10.8k`

### High

- yield: `95 q/ha`
- realized grape price: `EUR 120/q`
- annual opex: `EUR 5,200/ha`
- annual vineyard contribution: about `EUR 18.9k`

The base case is already reasonably constructive. It assumes the vineyard is recoverable and professionally run.

## 3. Olive operating assumptions

I modeled the olive area as oil production with no tourism premium.

### Downside

- oil yield sold: `450 kg/ha`
- realized oil price: `EUR 7.0/kg`
- annual opex: `EUR 3,600/ha`
- annual olive contribution: about `negative EUR 1.3k`

### Base

- oil yield sold: `650 kg/ha`
- realized oil price: `EUR 8.0/kg`
- annual opex: `EUR 3,200/ha`
- annual olive contribution: about `EUR 5.9k`

### High

- oil yield sold: `850 kg/ha`
- realized oil price: `EUR 9.0/kg`
- annual opex: `EUR 3,000/ha`
- annual olive contribution: about `EUR 13.8k`

The downside case turns negative because traditional hill olives can become labor-heavy very quickly if recovery is weak or harvesting is inefficient.

## 4. Other land assumptions

### Arable land

- downside net contribution: about `EUR 0.3k/year`
- base net contribution: about `EUR 0.6k/year`
- high net contribution: about `EUR 0.9k/year`

### Woodland

I do **not** give the woodland a meaningful income stream.

Instead:

- downside: `negative EUR 1.0k/year`
- base: `negative EUR 0.5k/year`
- high: `EUR 0`

That reflects maintenance, fire-risk management, and the fact that woodland is mostly scenic rather than a real cash-flow asset here.

## 5. Support payments

I included modest direct support rather than aggressive subsidy assumptions.

Eligible agricultural area assumed:

- vineyard + olive + arable = about `7.288 ha`

Support assumptions:

- downside: `EUR 180/ha`
- base: `EUR 250/ha`
- high: `EUR 320/ha`

Annual support included:

- downside: about `EUR 1.3k`
- base: about `EUR 1.8k`
- high: about `EUR 2.3k`

This is deliberately conservative relative to best-case program stacking.

## 6. Fixed annual overhead

Small farms often look better on paper than in reality because overhead is ignored.

I included annual fixed non-field overhead for:

- insurance
- accounting
- agronomic supervision
- security / minor upkeep
- administration
- minimal rural compound carrying cost

Assumptions:

- downside: `EUR 10k/year`
- base: `EUR 9k/year`
- high: `EUR 8k/year`

## 8. Exit value

For an agriculture-only valuation, I used exit values anchored to productive land value, not to restored-villa optionality.

### Exit value assumptions

- high: `EUR 500k`

These numbers reflect:

The `10%` hurdle is appropriate for a small, operationally messy, agriculture-only project with real execution risk.

## IRR at a `EUR 350k` purchase price

Assuming the buyer also funds transaction costs and recovery capex:

### Downside

- 5-year IRR: about `negative 9.4%`
- 10-year IRR: about `negative 6.1%`

### Base

- 5-year IRR: about `negative 0.5%`
- 10-year IRR: about `1.3%`

### High

- 5-year IRR: about `8.9%`
- 10-year IRR: about `7.9%`

This is the key result.

At `EUR 350k`, agriculture-only works **only** if:

- the vineyard is genuinely recoverable
- the olive grove is operationally better than the appraisal suggests
- the farm can be run tightly
- output pricing stays healthy
- fixed overhead stays contained

That is too many favorable assumptions for me to call it a comfortable investment.

## Purchase-price guidance

If the objective is a serious agriculture-only investment, my rough guidance would be:

- `below EUR 150k`: attractive enough to investigate seriously
- `EUR 150k-225k`: maybe workable if agronomic quality checks out
- `EUR 225k-300k`: only for a strong operator with a clear plan
- `above EUR 300k`: increasingly dependent on optimistic assumptions
- `EUR 350k+`: not attractive on agriculture alone unless you already know the farm is far better than it looks

## What would need to be true for `EUR 350k` to make sense

To justify `EUR 350k` on farming alone, I would want most of the following to be confirmed:

1. the vineyard is in recoverable shape with limited replanting need
2. the olive groves are mechanizable enough to avoid ugly harvest economics
3. water access is proven and cheap enough
4. the farm qualifies for meaningful support payments
5. there is either:
   - unusually strong grape pricing, or
   - a direct-to-consumer olive oil route with real margin
6. the buildings can support farming operations without meaningful structural spend
7. overhead can be kept very lean

If several of those fail, the investment case degrades fast.

## Main reasons the DCF is not stronger

1. The productive hectares are meaningful, but not large enough to absorb overhead easily.
2. Traditional olive economics can be mediocre if harvesting is not efficient.
3. A neglected vineyard may need real money before it becomes a cash generator.
4. CAP support helps, but does not rescue weak farm economics.
5. The land has scenic and optionality value that matters for hospitality, but that value is not monetized in a pure agricultural DCF.

## Most likely decision

If I were underwriting this as a pure agricultural investment, I would not want to pay `EUR 350k`.

My practical posture would be:

- keep interest if the price can move sharply lower
- or keep interest if the thesis expands beyond agriculture into hospitality / lifestyle optionality
- otherwise pass

## What would improve the model materially

The DCF is only as good as the agronomic facts.

The biggest data requests now are:

1. actual vine condition and planted varietals
2. estimated olive tree count and condition
3. recent production history, if any
4. irrigation and water-source details
5. photos of the productive parcels
6. whether a local operator would lease or manage the farm
7. exact tax path:
   - private ownership
   - company purchase
   - CD / IAP status

## Bottom line

Agriculture-only is **not** the strongest lens for this property.

At `EUR 350k`:

- possible revenue: yes
- possible positive nominal outcome: yes
- strong agriculture-only investment case: no, not under a disciplined base case

The math says this property wants one of two things:

1. a much lower entry price
2. a wider strategy than agriculture alone
