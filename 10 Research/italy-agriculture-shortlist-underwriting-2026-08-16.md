# Italy Agriculture-First Shortlist Underwriting

## Brief

This note pressure-tests the top shortlisted Italian farm properties under a strict agriculture-only lens:

- purchase price below `EUR 500,000`
- existing house already present
- limited additional capex
- managed by hired local labor / contractors
- target breakeven window of roughly `5-10 years`

The goal is not to produce fake precision from listing copy. It is to estimate whether the economics can work **before** spending real diligence effort.

## Method

I used a conservative unlevered DCF structure:

- discount rate:
  - `10%` as the disciplined base hurdle
  - `8%` as a softer comparison case
- annual free cash flow:
  - crop-level net contribution assumptions
  - less a fixed owner-overhead drag for remote management, administration, insurance, routine repairs, and small reserve capex
- annual growth:
  - `2%`
- terminal value:
  - conservative agriculture-only resale view, not hospitality upside

This is intentionally stricter than a lifestyle-buyer framework.

## Crop assumptions used

- vineyards:
  - base net contribution around `EUR 3.5k-4.0k/ha`
  - upside turnkey / stronger quality case around `EUR 5.0k+/ha`
- olives:
  - around `EUR 2.0k-2.5k/ha`
- citrus:
  - around `EUR 2.5k-2.8k/ha` base
  - higher only if irrigation, yield, and commercialization are genuinely strong
- arable:
  - around `EUR 0.45k-0.5k/ha`
- support / subsidy:
  - roughly `EUR 250/ha`

## Property-by-property view

## 1. Chiaramonte Gulfi, Sicily

- asking price: `EUR 205,000`
- house: about `110 sqm`
- land: about `6 ha`
- production described: orange grove + olive grove in full production
- water: drilled wells + artificial water reserve

### Why it remains the best screen fit

- lowest entry price relative to productive land
- explicit irrigation / water infrastructure
- crop mix is better than weak olive-only land
- existing house is present without the price looking dominated by residential value

### Base underwriting view

Because the exact hectare split is not disclosed, I modeled a reasonable mixed-orchard case.

- annual free cash flow:
  - base case about `EUR 10.5k`
  - upside case about `EUR 14.5k`
- purchase price justified by DCF:
  - `10%` hurdle:
    - about `EUR 144k` on a 5-year base case
    - about `EUR 171k` on a 10-year upside case
  - `8%` hurdle:
    - about `EUR 156k` on a 5-year base case
    - about `EUR 196k` on a 10-year upside case

### Interpretation

This is the **closest** listing to working on a strict agriculture-only basis, but even here the current price is only comfortable if the orchard economics are genuinely strong.

If the real production history is good, this is still the best candidate to pursue.

    - about `EUR 296k` on a 5-year base case

- yields are genuinely strong

It is a better farm than many alternatives, but not an obvious value at the asking price.

## Main conclusion

The broader screen result survives the deeper pass:

- there are some plausible candidates
- but **none** are obvious slam-dunk agriculture-only winners at current asking prices under a disciplined DCF

The closest fit remains `Chiaramonte Gulfi` because:

- the entry price is much lower
- water is explicit
- productive orchard land appears to be the real asset, not just the house

## Best next step

For the top `2-3` listings, the only way to move from screening to actual conviction is to ask the agents for:

1. annual production by crop for the last `3-5` years
2. exact cultivated hectares by crop
3. irrigation and water-right details
4. annual labor and contractor costs
5. subsidy receipts
6. equipment list and condition
7. confirmation of whether the house is truly habitable without meaningful capex
