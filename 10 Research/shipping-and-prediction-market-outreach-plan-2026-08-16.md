# Shipping and Prediction Market Outreach Plan

## Purpose

Create the human-network plan that supports the two research programs without waiting for code to exist first.

The goal is not generic networking.

It is targeted information extraction that improves:

- market selection
- variable choice
- data-vendor decisions
- execution realism
- compliance awareness
- eventual capital or partner options

## Principle

Ask each person only what they are structurally positioned to know.

Do not ask a broker for data-engineering advice.
Do not ask a quant for operational chartering nuance.
Do not ask a compliance or recruiting contact for shipping-market interpretation.

## Part I - Shipping outreach map

## Contact categories

### 1. Physical operators or chartering practitioners

Best for:

- which routes actually matter
- what vessel availability really means in practice
- when ballast counts are misleading
- how congestion should be interpreted
- what prompt tightness feels like before screens fully move

### 2. Brokers

Best for:

- where real screen liquidity is
- what nearby spreads are actually tradeable
- whether route or basket expressions behave cleanly
- how much broker color matters versus published benchmarks

### 3. Shipping analysts or strategists

Best for:

- segment ranking
- route-family structure
- data-provider realism
- whether Capesize versus Panamax versus tanker has the best first research odds

### 4. Data vendors or analytics providers

Best for:

- AIS cleaning approach
- cargo-flow features
- vessel classification issues
- proof-of-concept data availability and pricing

### 5. Potential capital or platform partners

Best for later:

- whether a serious pilot could eventually be capitalized or hosted

## Shipping outreach objectives

### Objective A

Pressure-test the current recommendation:

- Capesize first
- Panamax second
- tanker later unless contacts strongly point otherwise

### Objective B

Identify the smallest real data stack needed for a first pilot.

### Objective C

Learn which nearby FFA expressions are truly executable in practice.

## Shipping conversation prompts

### For operators or chartering people

- If you wanted to know whether the Capesize market was truly tightening in the next 1 to 2 weeks, what variables would you trust most?
- What are the biggest ways public AIS-based vessel counts mislead people?
- How would you distinguish real congestion from optical congestion?
- Which route family is most economically interpretable for a first systematic model?

### For brokers

- Which nearby contracts or spreads are genuinely liquid enough for repeatable trading?
- Is `M1-M2` the cleanest first expression, or are route spreads or route-versus-basket trades actually easier?
- Which signals does the market absorb immediately, and which get underpriced?

### For analysts or data people

- Which segment has the best balance of tradable liquidity, clean cargo anchors, and obtainable data?
- Which vendors are actually useful for a lean first build?
- What is the first feature family you would build if testing prompt tightness?

## Shipping contact tracker template

For each contact, capture:

- name
- role
- segment focus
- relationship strength
- what they can uniquely answer
- date contacted
- key takeaways
- follow-up needed

## Part II - Prediction-market outreach map

## Contact categories

### 1. Venue-knowledgeable traders or builders

Best for:

- venue mechanics
- execution reality
- data oddities
- hidden frictions

### 2. External-anchor quants

Best for:

- mapping threshold contracts to options or rates
- calibration pitfalls
- risk-neutral versus physical-probability translation

### 3. Compliance-aware finance contacts

Best for:

- whether prediction-market activity tends to trigger restrictions in bank settings
- how firms usually view event contracts, crypto-linked products, and bots

## Prediction-market outreach objectives

### Objective A

Choose first venue and first contract family for live research.

### Objective B

Learn where paper edge survives actual fees, spreads, and latency.

### Objective C

Clarify whether the best v1 is:

- Kalshi-first
- Polymarket-first
- or clean Kalshi semantics plus selective Polymarket structural overlays

## Prediction-market conversation prompts

### For venue-savvy people

- Which internal-consistency relationships persist long enough to matter after cost?
- Are Kalshi threshold ladders cleaner than Polymarket structures for a first build?
- What parts of Polymarket create the most false-positive "arbitrage" signals?

### For anchor-focused quants

- Which family is best for v1: BTC thresholds, Fed decisions, CPI ranges, or index thresholds?
- What is the most common mistake when converting option or rates data into event probabilities?
- Which calibration layer is non-negotiable before trusting an anchor residual?
