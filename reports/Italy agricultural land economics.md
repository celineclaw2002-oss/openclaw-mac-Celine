# Optional Houses Expand Leads, Not Returns

**Removing the house requirement materially improves the search universe, but it does not produce a defensible 5–10 year agriculture-only investment.** The best new leads are Montalto Uffugo, where €170,000 buys roughly nine planted hectares plus a cellar, and Soriano nel Cimino, where €145,000 buys 6.64 ha of productive chestnut and 1.26 ha of olives. Yet Montalto's attractive preliminary payback was built from gross-margin-style vineyard and olive assumptions that are too optimistic for a remote owner using paid local labour, while Soriano's stricter best case still takes about 17 years on an all-in acquisition basis. Ramacca remains the strongest water-and-operating-scale benchmark, but its seven evidenced citrus hectares support only about €10.7k–€13.7k of crop operating margin before remote-owner overhead and renewal reserves—far below the roughly €39.8k annual free cash flow required for a ten-year payback. The correct result is therefore a **ranked broker-diligence list, not a purchase shortlist**. No candidate should receive an offer until the seller provides crop maps, three to four years of harvest and sales records, paid-labour or contractor invoices, water-right documentation, and AGEA statements.

## House-free screening finds cheaper farms, not proven profits

The prior house-required search concentrated value in mixed lifestyle assets and produced Ramacca as the leading scale candidate: €329,000 for about 14.5 ha of citrus, olives and arable land with an artificial lake. Allowing land-only properties uncovers assets with much lower entry prices per planted hectare. Montalto's ask equates to about **€18.9k per planted hectare** before assigning any value to the cellar; Soriano costs about **€18.4k per disclosed productive hectare**; and San Pietro Vernotico asks roughly **€13.8k per total hectare**. These are genuinely better physical-entry metrics than Ramacca's approximately €47k per evidenced citrus hectare, although that comparison understates the value of Ramacca's other land and water infrastructure. The new screen also surfaces Casalincontrada, a €95,000 three-hectare vineyard with a private well, and larger vineyard candidates in Marche. ([Montalto listing](https://www.risorseimmobiliari.it/cosenza/vendita-azienda-agricola-montalto-uffugo-5097467.html); [Soriano listing](https://www.trovacasa.it/annunci/vt-tc-1767-73261478); [San Pietro listing](https://www.risorseimmobiliari.it/brindisi/vendita-azienda-agricola-san-pietro-vernotico-4891920.html); [Casalincontrada listing](https://www.case-appartamenti.eu/terreni-agricoli/abruzzo/chieti/vendita/casalincontrada_vigneti_coltivati.php); [Ramacca listing](https://www.risorseimmobiliari.it/catania/vendita-azienda-agricola-ramacca-5043274.html))

The economic improvement is much smaller than the physical-price improvement. Agricultural listings advertise hectares, trees, tanks, wells and the adjective “productive,” but none of the top candidates publishes the seller's multi-year output, realised farm-gate price, paid labour, treatment, irrigation-energy, insurance or repair costs. That omission matters because CREA-RICA's operating-margin methodology allocates **all paid and family labour and farm-machinery hours** to each crop; it is consequently a more relevant public proxy for an absentee owner than a gross margin that assumes unpaid family work. ([CREA-RICA methodology](https://rica.crea.gov.it/APP/documentazione/?page_id=1333)) Under that stricter lens, the earlier assumption that ordinary vineyards contribute €3.5k–€5.0k per hectare and olives €2.0k–€2.5k cannot be treated as owner cash flow. For example, Sicily's oil-olive crop operating margin was negative in every year from 2022 through 2024, while Sardinian DOC/DOCG wine grapes ranged from -€102/ha to -€1,880/ha; those regional figures are not portable forecasts for Calabria or Abruzzo, but they demonstrate why planted hectares alone do not prove earnings. ([CREA AREA-RICA crop reports](https://arearica.crea.gov.it/report_d.php))

This reconciliation reverses the preliminary Montalto conclusion. Its earlier **4.7–7.5 year** listing screen credited optimistic vineyard and olive contributions, generic subsidy income and only a partial ownership-cost load. That is useful as a lead-generation test, but it is not defensible underwriting. The cellar, tanks and tasting room could support a profitable wine-processing or direct-sales business, yet this mandate is agriculture-only: no branded-wine, hospitality or owner-operated sales premium belongs in the base case until documented in transferable accounts. Montalto therefore remains the first call because its hurdle is comparatively low, not because current evidence shows it clears that hurdle.

## Consistent costs erase every ten-year case

The normalized model uses **1.21 times asking price** as the acquisition basis: the ask plus a 15% agricultural-land registration tax for a buyer who does not yet qualify as a direct farmer or IAP, plus a 6% reserve for buyer-side agency, notary, legal, technical, cadastral and translation work. The 15% tax comes from Italy's registration-tax tariff; the 6% is a conservative screening reserve to be replaced by written quotes and a notary's deed-specific allocation. ([DPR 131/1986, Tariff Part I, art. 1](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.del.presidente.della.repubblica:1986-04-26;131!vig=)) The operating model starts from crop-level RICA operating margin where a relevant regional crop observation exists, then deducts €6k–€12k per year for remote agronomic oversight, accounting/CAA, insurance, municipal/property expenses and minor legal administration, plus €250–€500 per productive hectare for pumps, irrigation, fencing, access, replacement planting and other lumpy renewals. These ownership deductions are screening policies, not quoted local costs, and deliberately avoid double-counting field labour already included in RICA operating margin.

CAP/PAC support is **zero in the base case**. EU income support depends on eligible hectares, active-farmer status, conditionality and national administration; a headline ceiling or generic per-hectare assumption is not evidence that this buyer will receive cash. Credit only the three-year average of actual AGEA payments after confirming entitlement transfer and incremental compliance costs. ([European Commission income-support guide](https://agriculture.ec.europa.eu/common-agricultural-policy/income-support/income-support-explained_en); [Italy CAP Strategic Plan](https://agriculture.ec.europa.eu/cap-my-country/cap-strategic-plans/italy_en)) Water receives similarly strict treatment: a lake, well or irrigation network counts operationally only after the seller supplies the legal concession or title, authorised volume, pump and utility history, irrigation map, water-quality test and drought/interruption record.

A ten-year simple payback requires annual recurring free cash flow equal to **10% of all-in cost**; five years requires 20%. The model gives no credit for owner labour, land appreciation, resale value, hospitality, branded processing or direct-to-consumer margins. Even this test is lenient: at an 8% discount rate, ten equal annual payments need to be roughly 14.9% of cost to recover the initial outlay without a terminal value.

## Attractive-looking alternatives fail on scale, crop mix, or evidence

Ripatransone is the most important weak lead outside the top five: €400,000 buys 12.87 ha described as predominantly vineyard, irrigated and mechanically workable, but the ad omits hectares by crop, vine age/variety, water source, yield and sales. Its all-in cost is about €484,000 and it must produce €48.4k post-overhead FCF for a ten-year payback. Without the vineyard split or accounts, it is economically weaker than Montalto and too expensive to prioritize. ([Ripatransone listing](https://www.trovacasa.it/annunci/ap-tc-29137-73020340)) Nardò has unusually good physical disclosure—13 ha comprising 8 ha arable, 4.2 ha olives, 0.8 ha mixed fruit, a 0.25 ha new irrigated greenhouse, permitted artesian well and rainwater basin—but its €330,000 hidden portal price must be reconfirmed and ordinary olive/arable economics cannot support the ask. Only verified, sustainable greenhouse profit could rescue it, turning the proposition into an operating horticulture business rather than passive land ownership. ([Nardò listing](https://www.risorseimmobiliari.it/lecce/vendita-azienda-agricola-nardo-5005972.html))

Chiaramonte Gulfi remains a useful low-ticket comparator at €205,000 for about six hectares of orange and olive groves with wells and a reserve, but even an impossible upper bound treating all six hectares as oranges yields only roughly €9.1k–€11.8k of crop operating margin before at least €6k of owner overhead; the actual olive share lowers the result. ([Chiaramonte listing](https://www.immobiliare.it/annunci/127807360/)) Paternò–Ponte Barca, at €380,000 for eight hectares and 2,700 irrigated olives, fails because commodity olives benchmark negative at crop level and no integrated oil-business accounts are disclosed. ([Paternò search](https://www.immobiliare.it/vendita-terreni/paterno-ct/)) Loceri, at €420,000 with about 5.8 vineyard hectares and excellent water infrastructure, fails as a commodity-grape asset under Sardinia RICA results; any winery or brand value lies outside the mandate. ([Loceri listing](https://www.idealista.it/immobile/28879889/))

Paternò–Poggio Bianco is worth preserving as a watch-list item because the prior sweep recorded €270,000 for 14 ha with irrigation and a lake, while the unusable building no longer matters. Its current page, crop map and availability were not reverified, however, and it would still need €32.7k annual FCF on the 1.21x basis. Castiglione di Sicilia SP81, previously €280,000 for seven citrus hectares and a shared well, remains a reject: observed orange operating margins cannot meet its approximately €33.9k ten-year hurdle. Floridia's 1.75 ha of young lemons, Serdiana's three currently planted vineyard hectares, Gallese's 1.16 ha hazelnut parcel without water, and Vallerano's 3.4 ha chestnut grove are too small to absorb remote-owner fixed costs at their respective prices. Matera's 30 ha and Collazzone's 41.5 ha look scalable, but their arable/pasture/woodland-heavy mixes have too little income density. These are useful reminders that cheap hectares are not the same as productive cash flow.

## Documents, not more listings, determine the next decision

Every broker should receive the same evidence request before a visit. The core package is the cadastral schedule and parcel-level crop map; exact productive and irrigated hectares; cultivar, planting year, density and missing-tree/vine rate; annual kilograms or quintals harvested in 2022–2025; buyer invoices or cooperative settlement statements showing realised prices; payroll, contractor, machinery, pruning, treatment, fertilizer, harvest, irrigation-energy, insurance and maintenance invoices; tax and municipal charges; AGEA/PAC statements and transfer terms; machinery ownership and condition; leases, easements, agrarian pre-emption rights and third-party use; and an explanation of material crop losses, insurance claims and phytosanitary issues.

| Candidate | Questions that decide whether to continue |
|---|---|
| **Montalto** | What are the vineyard varieties, ages and authorised designations? Is the farm currently producing grapes, bulk wine or bottled wine? Supply grape and olive harvest weights, buyer invoices, separate field versus cellar costs, water source and permits, tank/equipment title and condition, and any inventory or liabilities included in the sale. |
| **Soriano** | Is every chestnut hectare harvested, DOP-enrolled and accessible to mechanical collectors? Supply cultivar/age map, grade and rejection percentages, realised prices, contractor invoices, gall-wasp/blight history, slope/access map, water source and olive-harvest records. |
| **San Pietro Vernotico** | Split the 21 ha among arable, olive and almond; state tree count, age and condition; document Xylella status, irrigation rights and volume, annual yields and buyers, building legality/use, machinery, PAC receipts and any extra 10 ha terms. |
| **Casalincontrada** | Supply Montepulciano/Trebbiano hectares, planting year/density, DOC registration and yield ceiling, three cooperative settlement statements, all contractor costs, well concession and flow test, irrigation map and replanting needs. |
| **Ramacca** | Reconcile the total 14.47 ha into citrus variety/age, olive and arable blocks; provide 2022–25 yields and invoices, contractor and machinery costs, lake ownership/filling rights/capacity, drought record, pump electricity, irrigation map, equipment list and AGEA receipts. |

The recommended action sequence is deliberately narrow. **First**, send the standardized request simultaneously to Montalto and Soriano, with a ten-business-day deadline; these two have the best combination of low capital at risk and disclosed planted area. **Second**, contact San Pietro and Casalincontrada only if their brokers can immediately supply the crop split and at least two years of sales evidence, while asking Ramacca for the same pack as the standing benchmark. **Third**, enter seller data into the same cash-flow template, keeping subsidy at zero unless AGEA documents transferability, and price local agronomist/manager, commercialista, insurance and renewal reserves using written quotes. **Fourth**, reject any property whose normalized base FCF is below 10% of all-in cost or turns negative under a 20% crop-price or yield shock. **Fifth**, commission agronomic, water-right, title/cadastral and tax diligence and visit only the survivors; negotiate price after—never before—the operating evidence is reconciled.

## Conclusion

The house-free rule improves optionality mainly by reducing the amount paid for non-earning residential space. It does **not** change the central economic fact: small Italian commodity farms often compensate family labour rather than generate the return required by an absentee capital owner. The most promising route is therefore not “more hectares under €500,000,” but **low acquisition price plus a documented specialty crop, legal water, mechanised access and an existing local contractor/buyer network**.

Montalto and Soriano deserve evidence requests; neither deserves a valuation premium yet. If both sellers fail to produce accounts, the rational result is not to relax the hurdle—it is to wait for a distressed, fully documented orchard/vineyard or to reconsider the ten-year target. On current public evidence, **zero candidates defensibly meet a 5–10 year agriculture-only payback**.
